# Explainer video: brief and script (draft 7, fifth cut)

## Brief

- **Audience:** general Twitter/X users who may not know what an exoplanet or a transit is.
- **Goal:** viewers understand what exoplanets are and why they're hard to find, what was searched, how the signals were checked, and what came out. They should leave knowing these are *candidates*, and that the code and data are open.
- **Format:** 16:9, 1920×1080 at 60 fps, 223 s (needs X Premium; the free limit is 140 s). Captions are burned in, because most viewers watch muted.
- **Voice:** ElevenLabs, Allison (voice library), `eleven_v4` (`tools/narration.py`, `narration_v2.py`, `narration_v3.py`), sped up 4% without changing pitch.
- **Music:** "Percolating Cosmos" (Suno, made by the user; non-commercial academic use). Edited to the narration and lowered under the voice, to −22 LUFS before the master (4 dB louder than through v7) (`tools/mix2.py`).
- **Visual rule:** every chart, number and light curve comes from the repo's real data, with no AI-generated imagery. Candidates are drawn as planets (a small orbit around the host star; a lit ball on the size axis), never as points of light, which read as stars.
- **Never claim:** "new planets", "discovered planets" or "validated". The word is always "candidates".
- **Credit:** Eumemic only; synthetic voice. Pavel Rabtsevich is credited in the narration, with his DOI on screen, and tagged in the post.

## Facts checked

- 4,039,203 stars searched in the full-sky search; 7,459,491 signals; 17 LEO-Vetter tests; 798,769 inverted stars (two samples); 438 full-sky (tiers A, B, N) + 50 tier L1 + 58 earlier = 546 (main repo a00d883, after the Gaia limits on companions).
- False alarms from the inverted light curves, as a share of the clean SNR ≥ 9 signals: 46% at MES < 10 (left out of the candidates), 16% at MES 10–12, 2% above.
- Tier L1: a gradient-boosted classifier trained to tell the real search's signals from the inverted ones selects 69 of the 382 SNR 7.5–9 signals that pass every check (about 92% astrophysical, judged on the inverted sample it never saw); 50 also pass the binary, grazing and literature checks and TRICERATOPS with the Gaia limits (51 before, 11 October; TIC 94673748 became a likely binary).
- 343 of the 546 candidates (63%) are between 1 R⊕ and Neptune's 3.88 R⊕.
- Earth dims the Sun by 1 part in 11,900 for about 13 hours; from a random direction, Earth's orbit crosses the Sun only 0.5% of the time.
- Rabtsevich (Zenodo 22967456, CC BY 4.0) reports two transit-like signals on TIC 4206066, a nearby K dwarf, found in Sector 98 and traced back to Sectors 6 and 32; he claims no validation. The pipeline recovered both blind.
- "Up to eight years": Sectors 1–104 run from July 2018 to 2026.
- The funnel's 438 includes tier N, which came from the neighbour-only rejects through TRICERATOPS, so the funnel graphic needs a side branch rather than a single line.
